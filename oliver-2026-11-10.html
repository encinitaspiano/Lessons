Terms to Know

Start here. These are the music words from this lesson. Some come from Italian, the language of music, so you will see them in music from all over the world.

Seed practiceRod's Music Garden
The gentlest level. Put three marbles on one side. Every clean run moves one marble across. A mistake just means that run does not count; nothing comes back. You are planting three good runs. We did seed practice on measures 13 to 15 of Spinning Song.
Sprout practiceRod's Music Garden
The middle level. Clean runs move marbles across, but a mistake sends one marble back, only one. A little more pressure without losing all your progress. We used sprout on your minuet.
Bloom practiceRod's Music Garden
The hardest level, what we used to call diamond practice. You need three clean runs in a row, and one mistake sends every marble back to the start. When all three cross, the section has fully blossomed.
Micro sectionRod's studio
A tiny slice of music, sometimes just a few notes, that you loop three times by itself before expanding back out to the full bracket. In the minuet it was thumb on G, third finger on B, and just that little moment. It is harder to start, but it clears the spot that breaks the flow, like a musical plumber.
Red bracketRod's studio
The section marked in red on your page that is this week's focus. For Spinning Song it is the passage at the top of page three.
DownbeatEnglish
Beat 1 of a measure, the strongest beat. We started the lesson with the downbeat of measure 13 to the downbeat of measure 15.
DynamicsEnglish
How loud or soft you play. In the red bracket section the left hand is the voice to hear, so the right hand plays as softly as you can while the left hand stays present. That takes real practice, so we split the hands between us first.
Deep into the keysRod's studio
Pressing all the way to the bottom of the key with a full, committed touch instead of brushing the surface. When you went deeper on the B flat, the note finally spoke, and the whole section started flowing.
♭FlatEnglish
Lowers a note by a half step to the key just to its left. In color-shape it is a triangle pointing down, drawn above or below the note, never around the note head.
Color-shape: downward triangle in the note's color
♯SharpEnglish
Raises a note by a half step to the key just to its right. In color-shape it is a triangle pointing up, drawn above the note.
Color-shape: upward triangle in the note's color
♭E flatNote
The bottom note of your 2-and-4 pair in the red bracket. All the bottom notes in that stretch are E flats.
Color-shape: yellow triangle pointing down
♭G flatNote
The one note that changes in the red bracket: finger 4 slides down from G to G flat while finger 2 stays on E flat.
Color-shape: blue triangle pointing down
♭B flatNote
The flat in the 1-2-4 group, and the lowest note of the B flat, D, G chord.
Color-shape: pink triangle pointing down
♯F sharpNote
The top note of the C, D, F sharp group. You first called it a flat, then figured out that sharp means up, so the triangle points up.
Color-shape: green triangle pointing up
F and GNotes
The two notes where the red bracket begins, played with fingers 2 and 3. Both are white keys, so they get circles.
Color-shape: green circle (F) and blue circle (G)
C and DNotes
The two lower notes of the C, D, F sharp group. Both white keys, so circles. Circle the lower one a little to the left and the upper one to the right so the marks do not pile up.
Color-shape: red circle (C) and orange circle (D)
PatternEnglish
Something that repeats. Our character number 13, Patterns Power Pablo, knows patterns are everything. There is a hidden pattern between the little sets of notes in the left hand of Spinning Song, and finding it is part of your homework.
MinuetFrench
A graceful old dance in three beats per measure. You worked on your minuet at home this week, and we used sprout practice and a micro section on it. No metronome on this one yet.
Laughter yogaRod's studio
When something small goes wrong, laugh at it instead of scratching the itch. Missing a couple of notes is not a big-picture problem, and laughing keeps you relaxed enough to try again. You are a natural at it.
Fibonacci sequence and golden ratioItalian / math
Named after an Italian mathematician. Each number is the sum of the two before it: 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21. Draw squares with those side lengths and a spiral appears, the same spiral hidden in a shell, in your curled hand, and in the painting in the studio. Patterns are everywhere, including in music.

Seed, sprout and bloom

Diamond practice now has three levels, and they live in the Music Garden. Seed is the softest: you are just trying to collect three clean runs. A mistake means that run does not count, but no marbles come back. Sprout is the step up: a mistake sends one marble back, only one. Bloom is the full challenge: three clean runs in a row, and one mistake sends every marble back. What we did on measures 13 to 15 was seed practice. You now have a printout of all three levels to keep by your piano.

Pick the level that fits the spot. A brand new tricky section starts at seed. When it feels steady, move up to sprout, and save bloom for when you are ready to prove it.

Which hand sings? Right hand soft, left hand present

In the passage we looked at next, which became this week's red bracket, the left hand has the voice we want to hear, so the right hand has to play softer. You said that part is not hard, and the notes are not, but making one hand quieter than the other is. So we practiced it the honest way: you played the left hand while I played the right, then you played the right hand as softly as you could while I played the left. That is how you teach your hands to balance.

The red bracket: colors and shapes before fingers

This week's focus is the red bracket at the top of page three. Before playing it we took the time to write in every color and shape. White keys get a circle around the note head in the note's color. Sharps and flats do not get a circle: a sharp gets a triangle pointing up above the note, and a flat gets a triangle pointing down. When two notes are stacked, circle the lower one a little to the left and the upper one a little to the right so the marks stay readable.

Here is what we found, from low to high. The passage starts on F and G, green circle and blue circle, with fingers 2 and 3. Then it shifts to E flat and G: a yellow downward triangle for the E flat and a blue circle for the G, fingers 2 and 4. Then one note changes: the G becomes G flat, so a blue triangle pointing down, and finger 4 slides down a half step while finger 2 stays on E flat. All the bottom notes in that stretch stay E flat, so once it is written in you can assume it unless we draw something different. Then there is a 1-2-4 group where one of the notes is a B flat, pink triangle down. Then two chords: C, D and F sharp (red circle, orange circle, green triangle up), and B flat, D and G (pink triangle down, orange circle, blue circle).

Taking that time felt slow. That is the difference between what your ego wants and what your brain needs. When we finally played it, the moves turned out to be tiny: 2 and 3 on F and G, shift to 2 and 4 on E flat and G, drop the 4 to G flat, and then look how easy it is to get back to F and G, because the 3 is already right there. The B flat spoke once you went deep into the keys. You said it helped a lot, and I agree. You have a powerful musical brain, Oliver.

  • When you make a mistake in the bracket, do not start over. Keep working through it.
  • Play deep into the keys, all the way to the bottom.
  • Right hand only this week for the bracket. Play along with the recording I made, first the right hand alone, then listen to the left hand part in the second half.

The golden ratio: patterns you can draw

We talked about the spiral hidden in the painting in the studio, in a shell, and in the side of your curled hand. It comes from the Fibonacci sequence: 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, and the next one, 21, because each number is the two before it added together. We drew it as squares: a 1 by 1, another 1 by 1, a 2 by 2, a 3 by 3, a 5 by 5, an 8 by 8, and a 13 by 13 that just barely fit. Connect the corners and the spiral appears. The 21 would not fit on the page, so your job is to tape more paper on and see how big you can make it.

Your minuet: sprout practice and a micro section

You worked on your minuet at home this week and played it well, with a good way to stop. One note kept making your brain ask a question, so you circled it in its color. Whenever something gets you, that is the first thing to try. Then we did sprout practice: clean three times in a row, and a mistake sends one marble back.

There was a little pause in the flow, so we tried something advanced: a micro section. Thumb on G, third finger on B, and just that tiny moment, three times. It is harder to start in the middle like that, but once the loop was clean we expanded back out to the regular bracket and it flowed. Finding the little spots that break the flow and clearing them out is like being a musical plumber. Go on to sections three and four this week, no metronome on this one yet.
