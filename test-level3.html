Terms to Know

Start here. These are the music words from this lesson. Some come from Italian, the language of music, so you'll see them in music from all over the world.

♯SharpEnglish
Raises a note one key: up, to the right. Looks like a hashtag or tic-tac-toe.
Color-shape: upward triangle ▲ (a mountain), same color as its letter.
♭FlatEnglish
Lowers a note one key: down, to the left. Looks like a lowercase b.
Color-shape: downward triangle ▼, same color as its letter.
○Regular (natural) noteEnglish
A note with no sharp or flat.
Color-shape: circle ○ in its letter's color (F is green, G is blue).
StaccatoItalian
Means "detached." A tiny dot over or under a note: play it short, with a little wrist bounce.
LegatoItalian
Means "tied together." Play smooth and connected. The opposite of staccato.
SlurEnglish
A curved line over a group of different notes. It tells you to play them legato.
TieEnglish
A curved line joining two of the same note. Play once and hold through both.
TempoItalian
Means "time." How fast the music goes, set on the metronome in BPM (beats per minute). Your page two tempo this week is 90.
MetronomeGreek
"Sergeant Metronome": the clicking beat that keeps you steady, like the click a band hears in their ears.
Note valuesEnglish
Whole note = 4 beats. Half note = 2. Quarter note = 1. Dotted half note = 3.
CountsEnglish
Beat numbers written directly under the notes. Held or silent beats get a box and an X, because silence is still time.

Playing together with Sergeant Metronome

When a band plays on stage, each musician hears a click in their in-ear monitors. The click keeps everyone on time together, so each person's part weaves into the others like threads in a blanket. Practicing with Sergeant Metronome trains you to do the same thing, so someday you can lock in with a drummer, a singer, or Mr. Rod.

Sharps and flats

  • A sharp looks like a hashtag or a tic-tac-toe board. It moves the note up one key, which is also to the right on the piano. F sharp is the black key just above F.
  • A flat looks like a lowercase b. It moves the note down one key, which is to the left. B flat is the black key just below B.
  • In our color-shape notes, a regular note gets a circle. A sharp note gets an upward triangle (a mountain) and a flat note gets a downward triangle. The color stays the same as the letter: F sharp is still green, G sharp is still blue.

Ties and slurs

  • A tie is a curved line joining two of the same note. Play the first one, then hold it through both. It can be drawn above or below the notes; it means the same thing.
  • A slur is a curved line over a group of different notes. It means play them legato: smooth and connected.

Staccato and legato

  • A tiny dot under or over a note is a staccato: play it short and detached. Your wrist does the work, like a little bounce.
  • Legato is the opposite: smooth and connected.

How many beats?

  • Whole note (big open circle): 4 beats
  • Half note (open circle with a stem): 2 beats
  • Quarter note (filled in with a stem, stem up or down): 1 beat
  • Dotted half note (half note with a dot): 3 beats
A whole note tied to another whole note gets 4 + 4 = 8 beats. You play it on beat 1, then there are 7 silent beats where you keep holding and counting.

Writing in the counts (grunt work for your brain)

Writing in counts is like push-ups from a coach: not always fun, but it makes you stronger.

  • Write each beat number directly under the note where it happens, spread out so anyone could read it.
  • Beats where you are holding (not playing a new note) get a box around the number and an X above it. For a dotted half note on beat 1, beats 2 and 3 get boxes.
  • Why bother? Because silence is still time. Held and silent beats still get counted.
  • When you see a little plus sign between counts, say "and".

Roman Trumpets and Patterns Power Pablo

Measure 13 is the same as measures 1, 2, 5 and 6. Spotting patterns like this is Patterns Power Pablo at work: once you know one measure, you already know the others. Writing a letter reminder (like a capital D) above a tricky note also helps your eyes see what is coming.
