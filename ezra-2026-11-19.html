Terms to Know

These are the music words from this lesson. Some come from Italian, the language of music, so you will see them in music from all over the world.

C positionEnglish
Right-hand thumb on C with fingers 2, 3, 4 and 5 on D, E, F and G. Left-hand pinky (5) on the C below, fingers up to the thumb on G. Both hands found C position today, with no peeking.
Hold (X)English
The X above a note means hold it; do not play a new note on that beat. Mr. Rod rewrote the X and the 3 bigger and put a box around the hold so your eyes could see it.
DownbeatEnglish
Beat one of a measure. Each section ends ON the downbeat of the next one, and you stop right there. That one note is the finish line.
SectionEnglish
A two-measure chunk of the song plus the downbeat that comes after it. Section 1 is the first two measures, section 2 is the next two. We build the song two measures at a time.
Seed, Sprout and BloomEnglish (Mr. Rod's practice levels)
Seed: notes, fingers, note values and a steady beat. Sprout: add counting out loud. Bloom: add Sergeant Metronome. You read all three off the sheet today, and your reading was awesome.
Sergeant MetronomeEnglish (Mr. Rod's character)
The click that keeps the beat steady. Today he was set to quarter note = 62. Your keyboard at home has one built in.
TempoItalian
Means "time." How fast the beat goes. Ours is 62 beats per minute for Dreidel.
Arrow down / arrow upEnglish (Mr. Rod's markings)
An arrow pointing down means that note is played by the LEFT hand. An arrow pointing up means the right hand comes back. We drew one of each in section 2.
GEnglish
The top note of right-hand C position, played with finger 5. Also the one note the left hand plays in section 2.
Color-shape: a BLUE circle.
CEnglish
Where both thumbs start in C position.
Color-shape: a RED circle.
DEnglish
The note Mr. Rod wrote in with the arrow up so you would see it coming.
Color-shape: an ORANGE circle.
EEnglish
Finger 3 in right-hand C position.
Color-shape: a YELLOW circle.
FEnglish
Finger 4 in right-hand C position. On the keyboard, F is the white key just to the left of the group of three black keys.
Color-shape: a GREEN circle.
Measure MuncherEnglish (red light character number 12)
Like Pac-Man, he wants to eat every note, yum yum yum, and he tricks you into playing past the spot where you were supposed to stop.
Bite-size BopEnglish (green light character)
He knows where to stop! He plays the bite-size section and stops right on the downbeat.
Speed Monkey, Grumpy Goblin and Non-repeat PeteEnglish (red light characters)
Speed Monkey wants to go faster. Grumpy Goblin and Non-repeat Pete do not want to do it again. Red light characters are not bad; they just show up to the party too early, before the decorations are up.
Reticular activating systemEnglish
The part of your brain that decides what is important. When you made the arrow to the G way more obvious, your brain started a filter that says: do not miss this one.
PercussionEnglish
Instruments you hit: drums, bongos, and your own legs. Banging along to music is how you feel the beat in your body.

Hands covered, eyes on the music

Your challenge today, should you choose to accept it: find C position in the right hand and C position in the left, and then Mr. Rod covered your hands. Not cutting them off! Just cutting the flow of your eyes, so your intuitive wisdom and your musical soul can guide your fingers while your eyes stay up on the music. You are an intuitive player, Ezra, and this is how your eyes learn to follow the page.

The X on your sheet was hard to see because it blended into the 3, so Mr. Rod took the time to rewrite the 3, rewrite the X, and put a box around that hold. Taking the time to make the page clear is part of practicing.

Seed, Sprout and Bloom: what you remembered

You read it right off the sheet. Seed: notes, fingers, note values, steady beats. Sprout: all of that plus counting out loud. Bloom: all of that plus Sergeant Metronome. We started on Seed, but not for the whole song. Only a couple of measures, and a couple is two.

Section 1: G C C D D E C, hold

One, two, three, hands covered, naming the notes: G, C, C, D, D, E, C, hold, and the last note you play is the G on the downbeat of section 2. That is the finish line. Doing it again and again without getting grumpy is how music tests our patience, so we did it three times and you earned credit even when it was not perfect. In Seed you just flub your way through.

What finger plays that G? Five. You wrote the 5 right next to the note, where your eyes can actually see it, not off in the corner. Then we added Sergeant Metronome. Holy moly, that was impressive! But was it luck or skill? That is why we do it two more times.

Section 2: G G F E D, hold hold, G D

Now from the downbeat of section 2 to the downbeat of section 3. G, G, F, E, D, hold, hold, then the G in the LEFT hand, then D. The 2 and 3 for the beats should be written in between the staves, with the X's above them for the holds. An arrow pointing down says: left hand plays this G. An arrow pointing up with the letter D, in orange because D is orange, says: right hand, here comes D.

You saw the D but forgot the arrow to the G, and you said the arrow was really hidden, so you made it way more obvious. That is your reticular activating system making a filter. Three times hands covered, then three times with the metronome at 62. One wrong hand position got us once, so we shook it off and went again.

Three Legos or marbles by the keyboard: slide one over each time you make it through. Three across means that step is done.

The Measure Muncher and Bite-size Bop

Then we combined sections 1 and 2: from the beginning all the way to the downbeat of section 3. And the Measure Muncher, red light character number 12, showed up! Like Pac-Man, yum yum yum, he wants to eat every note and he tricked you into playing past the stopping spot. So that round did not count. His opposite is Bite-size Bop: he knows where to stop. Stopping exactly on that downbeat may not seem like much, but being diligent about it is going to make you really strong.

You made a good point: the Measure Muncher and even the Speed Monkey can be good characters too, because wanting to keep playing is a good thing. Mr. Rod agrees. The red light characters are allowed; they just try to come to the party too early, before the decorations and the yummy food are ready. First we work in sections. Then they get invited.

Howl like a puppy

Mr. Rod told you about a puppy who did not know how to howl until he just let it out. Then he did it. That is what the notes need from you: trust yourself, let go, and let it out. When you got excited and the hand position slipped, we just shook it off and tried again.

Drumming on your legs: feeling the beat

You banged on a drum for about two minutes, and then Mr. Rod asked: do you need a drum at home? Nope. Sit facing me and hit your legs. Why? Because it is not about playing the piano right then. It is about feeling the beat so you learn to play ON the beat. Put on music, drum along on your legs or a drum, and just feel it. Your piano at home has a metronome built in, which is pretty cool for when you are ready for Sergeant Metronome.

Keep an open mind and an open heart, and always create with much love.
