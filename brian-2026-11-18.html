Terms to Know

These are the music words from this lesson. Some come from Italian, the language of music, so you will see them in music from all over the world.

Chromatic scalefrom Greek chroma (color)
Every key in order, black and white, one half step at a time. Yours runs E to E and back, right hand only, with the 1-3 and 1-2-3 finger groups.
TriadEnglish
A three-note chord: root, third and fifth. E minor is E G B; G major is G B D; D major is D F sharp A; A major is A C sharp E.
Chord symbolEnglish
A capital letter alone (G) means a major triad. A lowercase m after the letter (Em) means minor.
Root position, first inversion, second inversionEnglish
The same three notes stacked three ways: E G B, G B E, B E G. Right hand fingering 1-3-5, 1-2-5, 1-3-5, and the same for every triad.
Voice leadingEnglish
How each note (voice) of a chord travels to the next chord. Optimized voice leading moves each voice as little as possible and keeps the shared notes in place.
Common toneEnglish
A note two chords share. Em and G share G and B; G and D share D; D and A share A; A and Em share E.
Optimized fingeringEnglish
The same finger pattern for the same shape every time, so your hand learns to trust itself. Inversions are 1-3-5, 1-2-5, 1-3-5 no matter which triad you are on.
Hand position (low D position)English
Left hand with finger 5 on D, so D E F sharp G A sit under fingers 5 4 3 2 1. The whole Gone Away bass line is under your hand: E (4), G (2), D (5), A (1).
Key (key signature)English
The scale a song lives in, shown by its sharps or flats. Two sharps, F sharp and C sharp, means D major.
Intervalfrom Latin intervallum (space between)
The distance between two notes. The three to own like the back of your hand: major thirds, minor thirds and perfect fifths.
Minor third (m3)English
Two unplayed keys between the notes, for example D to F.
Major third (M3)English
Three unplayed keys between the notes, for example C to E, or D to F sharp.
Perfect fifth (P5)English
Six unplayed keys between the notes, for example C to G. Easy to miscount, so play it and listen too.
♯SharpEnglish
Raises a note one half step. F sharp and C sharp are the two sharps of D major, the key of Gone Away.
Color-shape: a triangle pointing UP in the color of the white note below it. C sharp is a red triangle pointing up; F sharp is a green triangle pointing up.
♭FlatEnglish
Lowers a note one half step. The same black key has two names: C sharp is also D flat.
Color-shape: a triangle pointing DOWN in the color of the white note above it. D flat is an orange triangle pointing down; B flat is a pink triangle pointing down.
TempoItalian
Means "time." The speed of the beat in beats per minute. Layer one of Gone Away is quarter note at 85.
Power areaRod's term
The chord zone just below middle C, roughly between G and D, where triads sound full. When there is a bass player, the piano stays up here and out of the bass's territory.

Warm-up: the chromatic scale and the inverted muscle

You brought the chromatic scale from 62 up to 94 over the week, and today with the click you ran it clean at about 120; without the click Rod heard you closer to 130. It kicked you a little at first and then it did not, and that is the whole lesson in miniature.

At the gym, two sets of a hundred leg extensions get harder as you go; muscles fatigue with repetition. The mind works the other way around. With music, each repetition should get a little easier, if you practice at the right ratio. Rod calls the mind an inverted muscle. The valley of disappointment from Atomic Habits is the same idea: we expect growth to be a straight line, it dips and wanders for a while, and then it takes off. Consistency is what carries you across. And the question underneath it all: do you consider yourself a musician? Identity is what makes the ritual stick.

Gone Away: finding the chords by ear

You chose Gone Away by The Offspring, the 2021 piano version. We listened, and you went straight for the bass notes, which is exactly right: the bass carries the roots, and once you have the roots the rest of the puzzle is mostly filled in. You found E, G, something on D, and A.

With a triad on top of each bass note: E minor, G major, D major, A major. Written as chord symbols, a capital letter alone means major, and a lowercase m means minor, so Em is the only minor chord of the four. Writing it down matters. Be a musical sketch artist: the act of writing is part of building a connection with the song, and emotion plus writing is what locks a memory in.

The secret of all secrets: voice leading

Imagine three singers, Sam, Gary and Paul, each holding one note of E minor. To get to G major, only one of them needs to move if you choose the right stacking of the chord. That is optimized voice leading: all well-written music has it, and it is what makes chord changes sound professional.

The tool for finding it is the common tone, the note two chords share. Em and G share G and B. G and D share D. D and A share A. And coming back around, A and Em share E. There is always at least one note in common, so the goal is to find the inversion of the next chord that keeps that shared note right where it is. That takes time to see, and today was the start.

Layer one: left hand in low D position, right hand in root position

Left hand: put finger 5 on the D below middle C and leave it there. E is finger 4, G is finger 2, D is finger 5, A is the thumb. The whole bass line sits under your hand, so the left hand never has to think or move; it just activates the right button. That is optimized fingering, the cousin of optimized voice leading.

Right hand, for now, all four triads in root position: E G B, then up to G B D, then down (quite a ways, lower than you might guess) to D F sharp A, then back up to A C sharp E. Rod recorded it two ways at quarter note 85: once as an arpeggiated pattern, bass note then the triad notes flowing through 1 and 2 and 3 and 4 and, and once straight so you can count along. Eventually you will count out loud as you play; that will be a humbling extra layer.

When you dropped a note mid-pattern you kept going, and that was the right call. You know this from the bandstand: nobody stops the band. You move through the mistake as the grid of time keeps rolling. Music teaches you to transcend good and bad; it just is.

Rod also showed what it will sound like later with voice leading in the power area, just below middle C: E minor, then G major by moving only the thumb down a step, then D major in root position because it shares the D, then A major. A common tone every time.

The inversions exercise

Start E minor an octave lower, below middle C, and climb: root position E G B with 1-3-5, first inversion G B E with 1-2-5, second inversion B E G with 1-3-5, and back to root position an octave up with 1-3-5. Four positions, even though there are only three inversions, because you land back home.

First inversion is the only one with a finger 2 in the middle. The notes are different for every triad, but the fingering is identical, because the shape under your hand is the same. That consistency is what lets you trust your body. Do the same climb for G, D and A.

You said you are a visual learner, so write the inversions out on graph paper: E G B, G B E, B E G, E G B, and so on for each chord. If your mind cannot see it or hear it, your fingers will never find it. Fingers are just an expression of the mind.

What key is Gone Away in?

E minor has no sharps or flats; G major has none. D major needs an F sharp; A major needs a C sharp. Two sharps, F sharp and C sharp, means D major. And look at how neatly it lines up: the two sharps of the key are exactly the two the chords needed.

In D major, D is the one chord, so E minor is the two chord, G is the four, and A is the five. Gone Away starts on the two chord: ii, IV, I, V.

The C major matrix: the hidden architecture

You played a C major scale up and down, then the same fingering starting on D with F sharp and C sharp, and there was D major. The matrix sheet is a whole chapter on one page. Fill in the scale: do is C, re is D, and so on. Then, with the upward arrows, write the root, third and fifth of each triad: C E G is the one chord, D F A is the two chord, and so on through the scale. Then label each one: C major, D minor, E minor, and so on.

Once you understand how triads are built from a scale, every song gets simpler. Figure out the key, and the chords stop being mystical. After C there are eleven more keys, but they all follow this same architecture.

Intervals and the color system

An interval is the distance between two notes. The three to own like nobody's business: major thirds, minor thirds and perfect fifths. A minor third has two unplayed keys in between, like D to F. Students see white-to-white and assume C to E is the same thing, but C to E has three unplayed keys: that is a major third. To turn D to F into a major third, raise the F to F sharp. A perfect fifth, C to G, has six unplayed keys; you counted five and missed one, which is exactly why counting is only the double check. Play the interval and listen. You already have a good ear; one that sounded minor was actually major because the bass of the piano darkened it.

The color system goes back to Isaac Newton. Each white note has a color: C red, D orange, E yellow, F green, G blue, A purple, B pink. A sharp is a triangle pointing up in the color of the note below it, so C sharp is a red triangle pointing up. That same key is also D flat, an orange triangle pointing down. B flat is a pink triangle pointing down, not a circle.

Your sheets: fill in the seven colors; find the perfect fifth up from each note (from C it is G), write it, circle it in color, and play root then fifth every time; then the sheet of all the major thirds and minor thirds. You have the erasable set, so goofs are no problem.

Keep the engine running

Keep the C major scale worksheet and the chromatic scale as warm-ups, and bring both sheets next time. In Note Rush, aim for level three, where the staff expands from C to C plus the low F and the high G. When a note pops up, play it and say its name out loud. Naming it feels harder, and that is the point: it records the note into your subconscious far deeper than just finding the key. Under a minute in two weeks and Rod will be thoroughly impressed.

Keep an open mind and an open heart, and always create with much love.
