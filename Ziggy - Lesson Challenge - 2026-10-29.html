Terms to Know

Start here. These are the music words from this lesson. Some come from Italian, the language of music, so you will see them in music from all over the world.

Middle C positionEnglish
Both thumbs share middle C at the center of the keyboard. The right hand plays C, D, E, F, G going up. The left hand plays the notes going down from middle C.
Color-shape: C = red circle, D = orange circle, E = yellow circle, F = green circle, G = blue circle
Quarter noteEnglish
A filled-in note head with a stem. It gets one beat. The metronome clicks once for every quarter note.
4/4 timeEnglish
A time signature that means four quarter-note beats in every measure. Count 1, 2, 3, 4 for each measure.
MeasureEnglish
A section of music between two bar lines. In 4/4 time, each measure has room for four beats.
Bass clefEnglish
The clef for lower notes, played by the left hand. It has two dots that sit on either side of the F line. Also called the F clef.
TempoItalian
How fast or slow the music goes, measured in beats per minute. Our tempo for Jingle Bells is quarter note = 65.
RestEnglish
A moment of silence in the music. Silent beats are shown as boxes in your music. During a rest, you do not play, but you still count.

Jingle Bells in middle C position

Jingle Bells is your first song in middle C position. Both thumbs sit on middle C, and your fingers cover C, D, E, F, and G. The right hand plays the melody going up from middle C, and the left hand covers notes going down. Our tempo is quarter note = 65, which means the metronome clicks 65 times in one minute.

Sing the words along with your playing. When you see an X, say "hold" out loud. Singing and saying "hold" keeps your brain connected to the music.

Reading and coloring your music

We used the color-shape system to mark every note in the piece. Each note name has its own color: C is red, D is orange, E is yellow, F is green, and G is blue. A circle means the note is on a white key (a natural note). An upward triangle means sharp and a downward triangle means flat. For Jingle Bells, all the notes are on white keys, so every note gets a circle.

Coloring the notes helps your brain connect the name on the page to the key on the piano. It turns reading music into something you can see at a glance.

Counting and symbols

In 4/4 time, every measure gets four beats. Write the counts 1, 2, 3, 4 under the notes so you always know where you are in the measure.

Three special symbols show up in your music. An X above a note means hold: keep that note down and say "hold" out loud. A squiggly line means the note is being held from before. Boxes mean silent beats: your hands lift off the keys and you count the silence. Notes that are circled together are played at the same time.

  • X = hold (say "hold" out loud)
  • Squiggly line = the note is still being held
  • Boxes = silent beats (lift your hands and count)
  • Circled notes = play them together at the same time

Self-control and the musical architect

Learning music takes patience and self-control. A musical architect builds a piece the same way you build a house: foundation first (reading the notes), then the walls (counting and rhythm), then the roof (playing at speed). When we skip ahead and try to play fast before the foundation is solid, the house wobbles. The patience to go slowly and get each step right is the most powerful thing a musician can have.

Chant of the Monks: your independent challenge

Chant of the Monks is a piece you are going to prepare on your own before the next lesson. Here is the process: write the counts under every note, color the notes using the color-shape chart, check the hand position from the chart at the top of the page, and then play it at quarter note = 65. Use the bongo drums first: tap the rhythm on a flat surface before you touch the piano keys.

Bongo drums

Before playing any piece on the piano, tap the rhythm on a drum or a flat surface. No notes, no keys, just the pattern of sounds and silences. This strips away everything except the timing, so you can hear the rhythm clearly on its own. If you can tap it, you can play it.
