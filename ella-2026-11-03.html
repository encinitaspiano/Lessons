Terms to Know

Start here. These are the music words from this lesson. Some come from Italian, the language of music, so you will see them in music from all over the world.

Metronome (Sergeant Metronome)Greek (metron = measure)
Rod's strict helper who gives a beat that never changes.
TempoItalian
Literally "time." How fast or slow the beat is. We measure it in clicks per minute on the metronome.
Bass clefEnglish
The symbol for lower notes, usually played by the left hand. Treble clef is for higher notes.
Treble clefEnglish
The symbol for higher notes, usually played by the right hand. (Treble means high.)
Whole noteEnglish
A note that lasts four beats in 4/4 time. It is an open oval with no stem.
Quarter noteEnglish
A note that gets one beat in 4/4 time.
Rest (squiggly)English
A symbol for silence. In your counts, a silent beat gets a box around it.
Measure and bar lineEnglish
A measure is one group of beats. A bar line is the vertical line between measures. Draw one after every four beats in 4/4.
4/4 timeEnglish
Four beats in every measure, and the quarter note gets one beat.
Hand positionEnglish
Where your five fingers sit on the keys. C position means thumb on C. E position means thumb on E.

Finger numbers with Sergeant Metronome

You said the finger numbers out loud while you played your finger exercise (1 2 3 1 3 1 3...). First we did it with your eyes closed. Your thumb tends to go a little too far when your eyes are closed, and that is normal. The speed monkey inside all of us wants to rush, but think of walking through a dark room: you slow down so you do not fall.

We went up in small steps: quarter note = 80, then 85. Your plan for this week is 80, 85, 90, 95 and no faster than 100.

Self-control means deciding to do something even when it is hard. Like the frog in slowly warming water, you build up the tempo a little at a time. Jumping straight to 100 is the chicken saying, "I want to skip ahead."

Note Speller page 12 and the bass clef

Page 12 is in the bass clef. You were asked to write a whole-note C on the staff and wrote it in the treble clef place. Before you write, name the clef. This is the bass clef. Pages 10 and 11 helped you find each note, and now you are learning to recall them without looking back. To know something is to be able to find it any time.

You also used deductive reasoning: if C is on this space, where is E? You counted up from your C guide and found E. Your best teacher is a mistake you learn from.

Colors: C red, D orange, E yellow, B pink. Write each whole note and circle it in its correct color.

Hand positions and the Rodeo duet

Before you played, you named the hand position for each hand, and I covered your hands so you had to trust your fingers. You named the notes out loud, then played with the metronome at 70, which is what you could really do, not what you wished you could do. Humans overestimate what they can do, so we stay humble.

Then we recorded the duet of Rodeo (lesson book page 49) with you one octave higher. You named some notes wrong but played them right, so what we say, think and do need to be in harmony. A second take is how every great song is made.

Counting and the Russian Folk Song

In 4/4, there are four beats per measure and the quarter note gets one beat. For the Russian Folk Song (lesson book page 48) you wrote the counts 1 2 3 4 under the notes, put a box around each silent beat, and drew a squiggly rest where it belongs. Finish writing in all the counts, boxes and squigglies and color every note before you play.

Then play with a slow, steady beat, starting at metronome 65, naming the notes and silent beats out loud. If it is solid, go to 70 or 75.

Conga line rhythm on the bongos

We learned the rhythm first on the bongos, with no notes, at quarter note = 70, switching hands. Then you placed your right hand in C position and your left hand in E position for "This Land Is Your Land," colored the notes (E, D, C, B, A each have their own color), and counted how many of each there were.

On theory page 28 you will write the counts and draw a bar line after every four beats. Then clap the notes while counting all the beats aloud with the metronome at 70. Page 29 is optional.
