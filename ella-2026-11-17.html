Terms to Know

These are the music words from this lesson. Some come from Italian, the language of music, so you will see them in music from all over the world.

MeasureEnglish
One box of music between two bar lines. Your section of Silent Night is the first four measures.
DownbeatEnglish
The first beat of a measure, the strongest one. "Four measures and a downbeat" means you play four full measures and then land on the first note of measure 5, and stop there.
Hold (the X)Rod's notation
An X above the music marks a beat where a note keeps sounding instead of a new note being played. When you see the X, you say "hold" out loud.
Middle C positionEnglish
Right-hand thumb on middle C, fingers 2, 3, 4 and 5 on D, E, F and G. Silent Night starts here.
D positionEnglish
Right-hand thumb on D, fingers 2, 3, 4 and 5 on E, F, G and A. You move here, just one note up, so your pinky can reach the A.
Finger numbersEnglish
Thumb is 1, then 2, 3, 4, and pinky is 5. The A in the right hand is finger 5, and the left-hand C on the downbeat of measure 5 is finger 1.
TempoItalian
Means "time." How fast the beat goes. Your tempo for Silent Night is quarter note at 72.
Metronomefrom Greek
Means "measure" plus "rule." The steady click that keeps the beat. I call him Sergeant Metronome, and he arrives in the Bloom level.
Gnote name
The bottom note in your left hand at the start of Silent Night. There are more Gs in the first four measures too.
Color-shape: blue circle (white key, so a circle; G is blue)
Bnote name
B as in boy. The note above the G in your left hand.
Color-shape: pink circle (white key, so a circle; B is pink)
Cnote name
The left-hand note on the word "all," played with finger 1 on the downbeat of measure 5. Red is the color that gets used the most, so it is the pencil that is always hiding.
Color-shape: red circle (white key, so a circle; C is red)
Dnote name
The very first note you play in the right hand, on "Si-" of Silent.
Color-shape: orange circle (white key, so a circle; D is orange)
Anote name
The high note your pinky reaches after you move to D position.
Color-shape: purple circle (white key, so a circle; A is purple)

Writing in the colors

Ella, we started Silent Night by taking the time to write in the colors. In the left hand, the bottom note is a G, and you found the other Gs in the first four measures. The note above the G is a B, as in boy. The left-hand note on the word "all" is a C, and red is the color that gets used the most, which is why the red pencil is always somewhere different.

One thing I noticed: you went to the answer really fast, before double-checking your work. Think first. Pause, look, and then say the note name. That little pause is where the learning happens.

Your section is 1, 2, 3, 4 measures and a downbeat. Not five measures. Four measures, and then the first note of measure 5.

Rhythm first, then the notes

Before playing, we said the rhythm of the first four measures out loud, with "hold" on the long notes. Then you played that same rhythm with the real notes on the piano. The first note in the right hand is a D. One, two, three, hold.

Wherever a note holds through a beat, there is an X above the music, and when we see the X we say the word "hold." I wrote the first X up top when we did the rhythm, and you wrote the other two X's in the first four measures the same way.

No luck in music

I told you about the day I found a $100 bill on the sidewalk. Nobody around, just sitting there. Was that because I did something, or random luck? Random luck. Now, if I walked out every day expecting another $100 bill on the ground, I would be disappointed and mad at the world. It happened once. That does not mean I can count on it.

Notes are the same. You cannot just randomly hope you hit the right key. You have to know what the note is, know what finger plays it, and then play it. That is the opposite of luck. That is being a good guide to Oakley, your musical brainchild.

The tiny move to D position

I circled a finger number on your page: finger 5 on the note A. From middle C position your pinky cannot reach A, so you move your whole hand up to D position. Yes, that early in the song! Thumb on D, pinky on A.

Here is the thing: it is a very small move. One note. Your brain kept telling you to fly way up the keyboard, and we had to be patient with it. Play "night," hold, hold, and then slide one note up for "all." We did it over and over until the move got small and calm. You do not need to complicate it.

I also drew a big circle around that spot, because it is the first time in the song that your right hand and your left hand play at the same time. The left hand plays the C with finger 1 right there on the downbeat of measure 5.
