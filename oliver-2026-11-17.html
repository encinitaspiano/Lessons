Terms to Know

These are the music words from this lesson. Some come from Italian, the language of music, so you will see them in music from all over the world.

MinuetFrench (menuet)
A graceful old dance with three beats in each measure. Your Minuet in G is one of your two harder pieces, and Sergeant Metronome is now at 65 for it.
StaccatoItalian
Means "detached." Short, bouncy notes that let go right away. Exercise number one is played staccato this week.
DownbeatEnglish
Beat one of a measure, the strongest beat. When I say "to the downbeat of 37," you play the first note of measure 37 and stop there. In exercise number one you name the downbeat notes out loud.
MeasureEnglish
One box of music between two bar lines. Your Minuet bracket is measure 3 to the downbeat of measure 5; your Spinning Song spot is measures 35 to the downbeat of 37.
Eighth noteEnglish
A note worth half a beat. Exercise number one is set so the eighth note clicks at 100.
TempoItalian
Means "time." How fast the beat goes. Minuet in G: quarter note at 65. Spinning Song felt really good around 80. Exercise number one: eighth note at 100.
Metronomefrom Greek
Means "measure" plus "rule." The steady click. Sergeant Metronome only shows up in the Bloom level. Sprout does not have him.
Half stepEnglish
The smallest distance on the piano, from one key to the very next key, black or white. In the Spinning Song spot, only finger 4 moves, by a half step.
♯SharpEnglish
Raises a note one half step, to the very next key to the right. The Minuet spot we isolated starts on the F-sharp.
Color-shape: F-sharp is a green triangle pointing up (sharps point up, same color as the letter F)
♭FlatEnglish
Lowers a note one half step, to the very next key to the left. In the Spinning Song you play finger 2 on E-flat.
Color-shape: E-flat is a yellow triangle pointing down (flats point down, same color as the letter E)
Octavefrom Italian "ottava"
Means "eighth." The distance from one note to the next note with the same name. In the Jumbo's Lullaby duet, my part was played one octave lower.
DuetItalian (duetto)
Means "a piece for two." Jumbo's Lullaby has a teacher part, so you played as written and I played below you.
Cnote name
In the Spinning Song spot, your thumb (finger 1) slides to C while fingers 2 and 3 stay put. You circled it red.
Color-shape: red circle (white key, so a circle; C is red)
Dnote name
The left-hand note near the start of the Spinning Song. You already had it circled orange, and we moved the finger number 1 away from the letter so it stopped looking like a P.
Color-shape: orange circle (white key, so a circle; D is orange)
Seed, Sprout, BloomRod's practice method
Seed: the friendly way to learn a tricky section, three times with no hiccups, mistakes do not count against you. Sprout: three times, a mistake sends one marker back. Bloom: the same, with Sergeant Metronome in the room.

Hanon and your two harder songs

Oliver, we started by going back to exercise number one. Your job this week: eighth note at 100, play it staccato, and name the downbeat notes out loud as you go. Then I asked you which two songs are the challenging ones right now. They are the Spinning Song and the Minuet in G. Last time we spent a lot of time on the Minuet, so today we started with the Spinning Song.

Before we played, a quick reminder about the Seed, the Sprout and the Bloom. When a section is really tricky, Seed is the friendlier way to learn it: you try it three times with no hiccups, and mistakes do not count against you. Sprout adds the pressure of a marker going back. Bloom brings in Sergeant Metronome.

How you sit, and how you breathe

You were sitting too far away. Stand up, look: the edge of the piano should be about one to two inches from your knees. Too close and you are a T-Rex. Too far and you are a zombie. You want to be smack dab in the middle. It is a subtle thing, but how you sit changes how you play.

When the grumpy goblin showed up about that left-hand D, we stood up and breathed: in through the nose, out through the mouth, five times. The first one did not count because of the attitude, but the next five did. Then you sat back down and sounded great. Really impressive. Spinning Song around 80 felt really good today.

  • On your page, the finger number 1 was written right next to the letter D and it looked like a P. We moved it over so the letter and the finger number stay separate.

Spinning Song: there is no luck in music

The tricky spot at the top of the third page is where your right hand has to move. First we played just the two notes with fingers 2 and 3, then jumped to 4 and 2. You even did it with your eyes closed. The left hand has its own move to 2 and 4.

Here is the lesson of the day. When you get to a spot where your hand has to move, you pause, move to the right spot, make sure you are on it, and then you hit it. Watch: I move. I find it. Then I hit it. What you were doing was hoping that luck would land you on the right notes. There is no such thing as luck in music. If you just hope it happens, it never will. So I said "stop" at the move, you found the spot, and then you played. Each time, you got closer.

The grumpy goblin does not want to hear that you are getting closer, but you were. This is the process, and the process takes patience. Where is your patience muscle? Not in your arm. It is in your heart, because when you love somebody, you have to be patient with them. Same with your music.

Then we added a little more. From the first spot, the only finger that moves is finger 4, and it moves by a half step. Then one more moment: finger 1 on C, finger 2 on E-flat, finger 3 on F. The 2 and the 3 are already there, so the only thing that moves is your thumb sliding to C. You circled that C in red.

This week: measures 35 to the downbeat of 37. Pause, line up the notes and finger numbers, then play. Add one measure at a time toward the downbeat of 40, or maybe even 43!

Minuet in G: Seed, then Bloom at 65

Your Minuet has a bunch of little red bracket sections. We started at the beginning of section 2 and played to the downbeat of section 3 three times at the Seed level. You said it felt "off key," and we figured out what you meant: it was a spot where the notes did not quite line up, not the beat.

So we isolated it. Starting from the F-sharp, we set Sergeant Metronome to 65 and played it three times. That is the Bloom level, because Bloom has Sergeant Metronome in it and Sprout does not. Then we went back to the start of section 2 and played into that same tricky part, three times at 65. Woo hoo, you were on fuego. I wrote on your page: quarter note at 65, dated today.

From the top with the metronome, we found the next spot: measure 1 to the downbeat of section 2, and inside that, measure 3 to the downbeat of measure 5. The fingering there is 2 in the left hand and 3 in the right hand, and that finger 3 crosses over. When the same note kept getting you, I erased the old finger number and you wrote a red 3 right underneath the note. Each round you got closer. As you learn to let go of the expectation and just embrace the repetition, it gets better. You were really owning it by the end.

  • Seed: three times, mistakes do not count against you.
  • Bloom: three times with Sergeant Metronome at 65. A mistake sends the markers back, and that is part of the process.
  • When a mosquito bites mid-measure, that one does not count against you either.
